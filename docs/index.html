      How the two parts are mixed

      Inside the pressure band, balls from bigger matches count a little more: each ball is weighted by the square root of its match weight divided by the average.

      Step 2

      Every match weight, and why

      A normal IPL league game is 1.0. Bars to the right make a match count more, bars to the left make it count less. The number beside each name is how many team-matches it applied to (one team in one match).

      Combining weights

      Match weight = biggest boost × second-biggest boost × the lowest discount (only if below 1). Only two boosts count, so stacking reasons can't run away.

      The cap

      A game that is not a final, playoff or semi-final can never go above —. No ordinary game should outweigh a knockout where the loser goes home (Eliminator 1.7, Qualifier 2 and semi-finals 1.75, finals 2.0).

      A-team events

      After everything else, Emerging and A-team games are multiplied by 0.7.

      Do pressure balls really play differently?

      Same innings, same over, same wickets down. Only the pressure level differs. On pressure balls, batters hit fewer boundaries and lose more wickets, as you would expect if the pressure is real.

      Step 5

      How a player is scored

      Each ball is compared with what an average player would make in exactly that spot: same phase, score, wickets, venue scoring level that season, how the others in the match did, and opponent quality. The gap is "runs added".

      Final score mix

      Each part is turned into a z-score within the ranked pool first, so the three parts are on the same scale before they are mixed.

      Pulling small samples towards normal

      k is the number of balls of "average" added to each player's record. A player with k balls of their own lands halfway between their raw number and the average. The pull is measured from the data: the less a stat repeats between two halves of a career (r), the stronger the pull.

      Other choices tried and why

      Opponent quality: adjusting for the strength of the batters or bowlers faced was tested and kept, because facing a strong line-up is harder.

       

      Reading the pitch from the opposition only instead of both teams was tested; the bowler top 10 barely moved, so both teams are used.

       

      Win impact (win-probability added) at 30% of the score was tested; it lifts finishers but repeats poorly, so it is left out. Recency weighting was dropped so every season counts the same.

      Limits to keep in mind

      Pressure performance repeats only weakly from one half of a career to the other, which is why every number is pulled towards the player's usual level. Ranks 4–10 can swap with small changes. Some players have most of their pressure balls in one competition. The data has no T20 World Cups, and Afghanistan's team ratings are partly rebuilt. The match weights are judgement calls, shown in full above so anyone can change them.
